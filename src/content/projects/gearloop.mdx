<h3>The venture</h3>
<p>Gear Loop is a mesh net that clips onto the outside of any backpack to carry the things that don't belong inside it: shoes, a jacket, a towel, a helmet. The product is simple on purpose. The class was about the business: our team of five ran it like a startup for a semester, with an operating agreement, customer discovery, patent research, a financial plan and two pitches. I was the CFO.</p>
</AlternatingBlock>

<div class="single-col wide">
<h3>Starting from the wrong end</h3>
<p>Our first idea was a clip to hang a wet jacket off a backpack, and we spent our early energy proving the clip worked instead of asking whether the problem was real. Reading <em>Bad Blood</em> for the class made that hard to ignore. Our professor kept pushing us back to the problem, so we went and talked to people.</p>
</div>

<PullQuote accentColor="#4f6b5a">
We had a solution first. The real work was going back to find out if the problem existed.
</PullQuote>

<span class="alt-block__eyebrow" style="color:#4f6b5a">Customer discovery</span>
<h3>What students told us</h3>
<p>We wrote an interview guide that never mentioned our product and logged 22 interviews. Of the 12 students we asked about carrying their own stuff, 10 said their bag can't always fit what they need. A follow-up survey backed it up and told us what mattered: if it swings or slips, people won't use it. That turned "a jacket clip" into a universal net that sits flat against the bag with a strap across the bottom.</p>
</div>

<div class="single-col wide">
<h3>Is there room in the market?</h3>
<p>We reviewed 15 patents and published applications plus 3 products already for sale. The closest, a ball carrier that straps to a backpack, is still in force, so our attachment had to avoid its exact combination of features. The closest products were helmet nets that only fit certain bags. Our angle: Gear Loop fits the backpack you already own.</p>
</div>

<span class="alt-block__eyebrow" style="color:#4f6b5a">My role as CFO</span>
<h3>Making the numbers work</h3>
<p>I built the unit economics and the financial plan. At $11.99 and about $5 to make, each Gear Loop earns $6.99, a 58% margin. Covering yearly overhead takes 3,737 units, or about $44.8K in sales, and our revenue plan passes break-even in year three. For market size, I worked from the global backpack market down to the customers we could actually reach first: CU Boulder students who walk or bike to class.</p>
</div>

<h3>Pitching it</h3>
<p>We pitched twice and showed a working prototype at the CU Design Expo, where judges scored us 48.2 out of 50. The pitch opened with the problem everyone recognizes: laptop and lunch crammed in the bag, gym shoes dangling off a strap, jacket tied around your waist. By the final pitch we had a real customer saying "I use it every day!" and an industry professional calling it a great idea and nicely made.</p>
</AlternatingBlock>

<div class="single-col wide">
<span class="alt-block__eyebrow" style="color:#4f6b5a">Takeaway</span>
<h3>What I'm taking with me</h3>
<p>Engineers tend to start with the thing they want to build. This class taught me to start with the person who has the problem, and to back every claim with interviews, data or a number someone can check. As CFO I learned that a product only works as a business if the margin, the market and the price all hold up together.</p>
