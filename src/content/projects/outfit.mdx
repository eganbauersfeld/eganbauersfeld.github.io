<h3>Overview</h3>
<p>I built a wardrobe app for my iPhone to keep track of what I wear and get outfit suggestions based on the weather. I wanted to stop rotating through the same few outfits, and the closet apps I tried were slow to set up, required accounts, and suggested outfits that didn't account for what I actually own. I wrote the spec, made the design and product decisions, and built it with Claude Code over three days and 19 versions.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div>
<h3>How it works</h3>
<p>The home screen gives a general suggestion based on the forecast, like "sweater + pants + jacket." Specific outfits from my closet are on the Ideas tab. They come from an on-device rules engine that checks each outfit against the hourly forecast, scores color combinations, and adjusts based on what I log and like. I first tried a version that used Claude's API to pick outfits, but it cost money every time it ran, so I switched to the rules engine, which is free and works offline.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="single-col wide">
<h3>Iteration</h3>
<p>I used the app every day while building it, and most of the changes came from that. The first version worked but I didn't enjoy using it, so I redesigned the interface. My clothing photos all had different backgrounds, so I added on-device background removal that puts every item on the same backdrop. The first model I used was too large and crashed my phone; switching to a 4.6 MB model and adding a crash guard fixed it. I also added support for logging more than one outfit per day.</p>
</div>

<div class="single-col wide">
<h3>Outcome</h3>
<p>Everything is stored on the phone, with no accounts or server, so it costs nothing to run and works offline. I still use it every day. The screenshots show a sample closet.</p>
